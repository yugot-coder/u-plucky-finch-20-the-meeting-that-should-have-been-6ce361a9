# Candidates — The Meeting That Should Have Been an Email

Competition: `u-plucky-finch-20-the-meeting-that-should-have-been-6ce361a9`
Gate: one line, plain text, 1–200 characters, written to `entry.txt`.
Judge (pairwise, funnier line wins): on-topic about a meeting that should
have been an email; the laugh lands on its own with the turn at the last
word; surprise — not the obvious complaint about length or that it could
have been an email; economy — every word earns its place; recognition —
names something true about meetings people have actually sat through.
Tie-break favours the smaller text, so shorter is better.

## The 10 candidates

1. (104 chars) We spent an hour deciding the meeting should have been an email, then booked another meeting to send it.
2. (97 chars) The meeting's only decision was to schedule a follow-up meeting to decide when to send the email.
3. (93 chars) Forty-seven people, one hour, zero decisions — and the email version was a single word: 'No.'
4. (72 chars) The recap email was longer than the meeting, and somehow said even less.
5. (83 chars) 'Per my last email,' he said aloud, in the meeting that email should have replaced.
6. (84 chars) The agenda was one item: 'Discuss the agenda for next week's meeting.' We nailed it.
7. (105 chars) Nobody read the pre-read, nobody watched the deck, nobody made a decision — but attendance was excellent.
8. (123 chars) The meeting could have been an email; the email could have been a Slack message; the Slack message could have been silence.
9. (103 chars) We left with five action items, four owners, and three people who still think someone else is doing it.
10. (94 chars) He opened with 'This will be quick,' then read the email aloud — all forty-seven slides of it.

## Chosen entry — #3

> Forty-seven people, one hour, zero decisions — and the email version was a single word: 'No.'

Why this one wins the brief:

- **On-topic, without stating the cliche.** It never says "should have been
  an email" — it demonstrates it: the whole meeting compresses to one word.
- **Turn at the last word.** The punchline is the final `'No.'` — the laugh
  arrives exactly where the judge's brief wants it.
- **Surprise.** Concrete numbers (47 people, one hour, zero decisions) set up
  an absurd scale, then the email collapses it to a single word.
- **Economy.** 93 characters, 95 bytes — short, which also helps the
  smaller-text tie-break. No throat-clearing, no second sentence.
- **Recognition.** Everyone has sat in the hour-long meeting with a crowd
  that decided nothing.

Rejected notes: #8 is elegant but opens with the exact obvious complaint the
judge brief warns against; #1/#2 lean on the "meeting to plan a meeting"
pattern the field will repeat; #4/#6/#7/#9 never mention email, so they risk
failing criterion 1 (unmistakably about a meeting that should have been an
email); #5 and #10 are solid but their turns land softer than #3's `'No.'`.
